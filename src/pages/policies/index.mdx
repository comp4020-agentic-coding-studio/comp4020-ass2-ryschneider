# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

Work submitted after the deadline loses 5% of the available mark per day,
capped at five days, after which it isn't accepted without a prior extension.
Extensions are granted for documented illness or misadventure — email the
convenor before the deadline where possible, or as soon as you're able to
afterward.

## Source citation

This course argues from historical evidence, so every factual claim in
Assignment 1 and the final project needs a source a marker could actually go
and check. A footnote, an inline citation, or a linked reference all count;
an unsourced claim about what a group "believed" or "did" doesn't, no matter
how confidently it's stated. Wikipedia is a fine starting point for finding
primary and secondary sources — it is not, by itself, an adequate citation
for a specific factual claim.

## Academic integrity

Submit your own argument, in your own words, built from sources you've
actually read. Quote and cite anything you didn't write yourself, including
material an AI tool helped draft — declare the assistance and what it was
used for. Passing off someone else's analysis of a splinter group as your own
independent research is treated as a serious integrity breach, not a
citation slip.

## Getting help

Ask during a session first — most questions turn out to matter to more than
one person, and answering them in the room is faster than an email thread.
Outside sessions, email the [convenor or tutor](/people/) directly; expect a
reply within two working days during term, and not to hear back over
semester breaks.
